> **In short:**
> 1. Invoicing software creates quotes and invoices, tracks payments and chases late payers.
> 2. E-invoicing compatibility has become a central criterion in France.
> 3. Integrations with the bank and the accounting tool avoid double data entry.
> 4. A trial period, when available, remains the best way to validate a choice.

## What invoicing software is for

**Invoicing software** automates the creation of quotes and invoices. It assigns continuous numbering to invoices, pulls in customer details, calculates amounts before tax and VAT, then tracks the status of each payment. It is a clear improvement on the spreadsheet, a frequent source of numbering and calculation errors.

Beyond saving time, the tool brings consistency. Mandatory mentions appear on every document, invoices are stored in the same place and a customer's history can be checked in seconds. For a small business, it is often the first management software put in place, even before accounting.

## Signs it is time to get equipped

Moving from a spreadsheet or word processor to dedicated software usually makes sense when several of these situations arise:

- **Invoices are piling up** and numbering is hard to keep error-free.
- **Reminders take time**: checking by hand which invoices are paid.
- **Several people invoice** and risk creating duplicates.
- **The accountant asks for exports** that the spreadsheet produces poorly.
- **E-invoicing is arriving** and the current format does not meet it.

## Essential features to check

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Quotes that convert into invoices | Avoids re-entering the same information |
| Automatic reminders | Cuts the time spent on unpaid invoices |
| Bank reconciliation | Shows which invoices have been paid |
| Accounting export | Makes the accountant's work easier |
| Multi-user management | Useful as soon as several people invoice |

Other features become useful depending on the business: deposit invoices, recurring subscriptions, multiple currencies, a product catalogue with prefilled prices and VAT, or online payment directly from the invoice.

## E-invoicing, a criterion in its own right

France is rolling out electronic invoicing between businesses. According to [economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/tout-savoir-sur-la-facturation-electronique-pour-les-entreprises) (page in French, consulted on 27 October 2026), the timetable is as follows:

- **Since 1 September 2026**: all businesses, whatever their size, must be able to receive electronic invoices. Large companies and mid-sized companies must also issue them and transmit their transaction data (e-reporting).
- **From 1 September 2027**: small and micro-businesses will in turn have to issue their invoices electronically and transmit their data.

An electronic invoice is not just a PDF sent by email. It contains structured data that software can read automatically, and it goes through an approved platform. Before choosing a tool, it is therefore worth checking how the vendor handles issuing and receiving, and which approved platform it works with, or whether it is one itself.

## A method for comparing tools

An effective comparison follows a few simple steps:

1. **List your needs**: invoices per month, quotes, subscriptions, number of users, currency needs.
2. **Check compliance**: mandatory mentions, numbering, archiving, e-invoicing.
3. **Test on real cases**: create a quote, turn it into an invoice, simulate a reminder, run an accounting export.
4. **Review the terms**: commitment, cancellation conditions, data export if you leave.
5. **Ask your accountant**: they often know which tools integrate well with their own software.

Prices change often and depend on the options chosen. It is best to check them directly on each vendor's website at the time of choosing, comparing what each plan includes.

## Integrations and security

A good tool connects to the rest of your environment: bank, [CRM]({{< relref "what-is-a-crm.md" >}}), accounting tool. The security of invoicing data is also a criterion: [cybersecurity best practices]({{< relref "small-business-cybersecurity-best-practices.md" >}}) apply to user accounts. Finally, some tasks such as reminders can fit into a broader effort to [automate repetitive tasks]({{< relref "automate-repetitive-tasks.md" >}}).

On security, three points deserve particular attention: two-factor authentication on accounts, permission management (who can create, edit or delete an invoice) and backups. Online software stores data with the vendor, which makes it useful to know where its servers are located.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing on price alone**: a cheap but incomplete tool forces you to add other software.
- **Forgetting data export**: changing tools becomes difficult if the history cannot be retrieved.
- **Overlooking the transition**: invoice numbering must stay continuous when switching.
- **Ignoring the accountant's needs**: an unsuitable export format leads to re-entry.

<summary>How do you choose invoicing software?</summary>

Start by listing your real needs: invoice volume, quotes, reminders, multiple currencies, number of users. Then check e-invoicing compatibility, integrations with the bank and accounting, and test the tool during a trial period when the vendor offers one.

<details>
<summary>What is the difference between invoicing software and accounting software?</summary>

Invoicing software issues quotes and invoices and tracks payments. Accounting software records all of a company's transactions and produces the financial statements. Many tools combine both, or offer a connection between them.

</details>

<summary>Is invoicing software useful for a freelancer?</summary>

Yes, as soon as invoices become regular. The tool numbers invoices automatically, keeps a history and makes reminders easier, which limits errors compared with a spreadsheet.

</details>
