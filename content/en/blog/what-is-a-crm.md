> **In short:**
> 1. A CRM centralises contacts, interaction history and sales opportunities.
> 2. It serves sales teams as much as marketing and customer service.
> 3. It becomes valuable once manual customer tracking gets difficult.
> 4. Integrations and ease of use matter as much as the feature list.

## What is a CRM

A **CRM**, short for Customer Relationship Management, is software for managing customer relationships. It brings together in a single database contact records, companies, interactions (calls, emails, meetings) and sales opportunities. The goal is simple: every team member sees the same, up-to-date information.

The term refers both to an approach and to a tool. The approach is to organise the customer relationship around shared data rather than personal address books. The tool makes that organisation workable day to day. A well-used CRM prevents a customer from being chased twice on the same day by two different people, or a request from going unanswered because it landed in an individual inbox.

## Why move from a spreadsheet to a CRM

Many businesses start tracking prospects in a spreadsheet. The method works as long as volumes stay low and only one person updates the file. Then the limits appear:

- **Multiple versions**: several copies of the file circulate and nobody knows which one is right.
- **Scattered history**: conversations stay in each person's inbox.
- **Forgotten follow-ups**: no reminder is triggered automatically.
- **Hard to steer**: figures must be rebuilt by hand to see where sales stand.

A CRM solves these problems by linking each contact to its history and scheduling the next actions.

## Main features

- **Contact management**: detailed records, segmentation, history.
- **Sales pipeline**: tracking opportunities stage by stage, from first contact to signature.
- **Tasks and reminders**: scheduled follow-ups so nothing is forgotten.
- **Marketing**: mailing lists, campaigns, interaction tracking.
- **Reports**: an overview of sales activity.

Depending on the vendor, other modules are available: quote creation, built-in telephony, support ticket management, lead capture forms on the website. It is better to start from the team's real needs than from the longest feature list, as each extra module requires configuration and training.

## Uses by team

| Team | How it uses the CRM |
|---|---|
| Sales | Track prospects and open quotes |
| Marketing | Segment contacts and measure campaigns |
| Customer service | Check a customer's history before replying |
| Management | Monitor activity and forecast revenue |

What these uses have in common is shared data. When customer service sees that a quote is pending, it adjusts its answer. When marketing knows which contacts have already bought, it avoids sending them a welcome offer.

## Building a clear sales pipeline

The pipeline is the core of a sales-oriented CRM. It breaks a prospect's journey into stages, for example: first contact, need identified, proposal sent, negotiation, won or lost. A few principles make this breakdown useful:

1. **Observable stages**: each stage matches a verifiable fact (a meeting took place, a quote was sent), not an impression.
2. **Few stages**: five or six are usually enough. Beyond that, salespeople hesitate over the right column.
3. **A reason for losing**: recording why a deal was lost helps improve the offer.
4. **A next action**: every open opportunity should have a dated task.

## A successful roll-out

CRM projects rarely fail because of the tool itself. They more often fail because of a rushed data import or users who see no point in entering their information. A few steps limit that risk:

- **Clean the files** before importing: duplicates, outdated contacts, badly filled fields.
- **Set simple data entry rules**: which fields are mandatory, how to name a company.
- **Start small**: contacts and pipeline first, marketing and automations later.
- **Train and support**: a short hands-on session beats a long manual.
- **Show the benefit**: a dashboard that helps the team encourages data entry.

## Personal data and the GDPR

A CRM holds personal data about customers and prospects. The GDPR requires in particular collecting only the information needed, setting retention periods and allowing people to access their data or have it deleted. In France, the data protection authority (CNIL) publishes resources on commercial prospecting and customer files. When choosing a tool, it is useful to ask the vendor where the data is hosted and how deletion requests are handled.

## Choosing the right CRM

Before choosing, it is worth checking connections with existing tools, such as your [invoicing software]({{< relref "invoicing-software-criteria.md" >}}). A CRM is also often part of an effort to [automate repetitive tasks]({{< relref "automate-repetitive-tasks.md" >}}), and it holds sensitive data that calls for applying [cybersecurity best practices]({{< relref "small-business-cybersecurity-best-practices.md" >}}).

Other criteria to review:

- **Ease of use**: a clear interface helps the team adopt the tool.
- **Mobile access**: useful for salespeople on the move.
- **Scalability**: the tool should keep up as the number of users grows.
- **Data export**: it must remain possible to retrieve your data if you change tools.
- **Trial period**: when the vendor offers one, it allows testing on real cases.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What is a CRM?</summary>

A CRM (Customer Relationship Management) is customer relationship management software. It centralises contacts, the history of interactions and sales opportunities, so that the whole team shares the same information and follows each customer without losing data.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is a CRM only for large companies?</summary>

No. Many CRMs are designed for freelancers and small teams. A CRM becomes useful as soon as the number of contacts makes manual tracking difficult, or when several people deal with the same customers.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the difference between a CRM and an email marketing tool?</summary>

An email marketing tool sends campaigns. A CRM manages the whole customer relationship: contacts, meetings, quotes, opportunities. Many CRMs include email features or connect to a dedicated tool.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How long does it take to set up a CRM?</summary>

It depends on the volume of data to import and the number of users. For a small team, most of the work consists of cleaning existing files, defining pipeline stages and training users. A gradual start, one feature at a time, limits friction.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Does a CRM have to comply with the GDPR?</summary>

Yes. A CRM holds personal data about customers and prospects. The business must in particular limit the data collected to what is useful, set retention periods and allow people to exercise their rights. Choosing a vendor that states where data is hosted makes compliance easier.

</details>
