> **In short:**
> 1. A CRM centralises contacts, the history of interactions and sales opportunities.
> 2. It serves sales teams as much as marketing and customer service.
> 3. Its value appears as soon as manual customer follow-up becomes difficult.
> 4. Integrations and ease of use matter as much as the feature list.

## What is a CRM

A **CRM**, for Customer Relationship Management, is customer relationship management software. It brings together in a single database contact records, companies, interactions (calls, emails, meetings) and sales opportunities. The goal is simple: every team member sees the same, up-to-date information.

## Main features

- **Contact management**: detailed records, segmentation, history.
- **Sales pipeline**: tracking opportunities by stage, from first contact to signature.
- **Tasks and reminders**: scheduled follow-ups so nothing is forgotten.
- **Marketing**: mailing lists, campaigns, interaction tracking.
- **Reports**: an overview of sales activity.

## Uses by team

| Team | How it uses the CRM |
|---|---|
| Sales | Track prospects and pending quotes |
| Marketing | Segment contacts and measure campaigns |
| Customer service | Look up a customer's history before replying |
| Management | Monitor activity and forecast revenue |

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What is a CRM?</summary>

A CRM (Customer Relationship Management) is customer relationship management software. It centralises contacts, the history of interactions and sales opportunities, so that the whole team shares the same information and follows each customer without losing data.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is a CRM only for large companies?</summary>

No. Many CRMs are designed for freelancers and small teams. The benefit appears as soon as the number of contacts makes manual follow-up difficult, or several people deal with the same customers.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the difference between a CRM and an email marketing tool?</summary>

An email marketing tool is used to send campaigns. A CRM manages the customer relationship as a whole: contacts, meetings, quotes, opportunities. Many CRMs include email features or connect to a dedicated tool.

</details>
