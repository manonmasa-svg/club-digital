> **In short:**
> 1. Two-factor authentication protects accounts even if a password is stolen.
> 2. Updates fix known vulnerabilities and should be applied quickly.
> 3. Regular backups, including an offline copy, let you recover after an incident.
> 4. Staff awareness remains the best defence against phishing.

## Why cybersecurity also matters for small businesses

**Cybersecurity** is not just for large groups. Small businesses use the same online tools (email, [invoicing software]({{< relref "invoicing-software-criteria.md" >}}), shared storage) and face the same common attacks: phishing, password theft, ransomware.

The most frequent attacks do not even target a particular company. They are sent in bulk and exploit the simplest weaknesses: a reused password, out-of-date software, an employee who clicks on a booby-trapped link. A small business, often without a dedicated IT department, can therefore be hit as easily as a large one. The consequences can be serious: business at a standstill, lost data, customers to notify.

## The most common threats

- **Phishing**: a message imitates a bank, a supplier or a colleague to obtain credentials or a payment.
- **Ransomware**: a program encrypts files and a ransom is demanded to unlock them.
- **Account takeover**: a password leaked from another site is reused to log in.
- **Fake supplier fraud**: a message announces new bank details to divert a transfer.
- **Lost or stolen equipment**: an unencrypted laptop exposes all the data it holds.

## Basic measures

| Measure | What it protects |
|---|---|
| Two-factor authentication | Online accounts if a password is stolen |
| Password manager | Prevents reusing the same password |
| Regular updates | Devices and software against known flaws |
| Backups with an offline copy | Data in case of failure or ransomware |
| Limited administrator accounts | The system if a workstation is compromised |

These measures do not require a large budget. Above all they require regularity: enable two-factor authentication on all important accounts (email, bank, management tools), schedule automatic updates where possible and check from time to time that backups can actually be restored.

## Organising backups

A backup is only useful if it allows data to be restored. The so-called 3-2-1 rule is a good benchmark: three copies of the data, on two different types of media, with one copy offsite or offline. The offline copy matters because ransomware can also encrypt backups that are reachable from the network.

A few questions help check the setup:

1. Which data is backed up, and which is not?
2. How often, and how many versions are kept?
3. Who knows how to restore a backup, and how long does it take?
4. Was the last restore test carried out within the past few months?

## Training and preparing the team

Most attacks start with a deceptive message. Learning to check the sender, to be wary of urgent requests and to report any doubt greatly reduces the risk. It also helps to know in advance who to contact in case of an incident: in France, the public platform [cybermalveillance.gouv.fr](https://www.cybermalveillance.gouv.fr/) supports businesses that fall victim.

One simple rule protects against fake supplier fraud: any request to change bank details is checked through another channel, for example a call to the supplier's number already on file, and never to the number given in the suspicious message.

## Responding to an incident

Preparing the response in advance avoids poor decisions in a hurry. The first reflexes:

- **Isolate** the affected devices by disconnecting them from the network, without switching them off if an analysis is planned.
- **Change** the passwords of the accounts concerned from a clean device.
- **Keep the evidence**: messages received, screenshots, logs.
- **Get support** from a competent provider and file a complaint if necessary.
- **Report** personal data breaches: the GDPR requires notifying the CNIL within 72 hours when the breach poses a risk to individuals.

## Securing tools and hosting too

The choice of [web hosting]({{< relref "shared-hosting-vps-cloud.md" >}}) affects the security level of the company website. Employee data processed by [payroll software]({{< relref "payroll-software-key-features.md" >}}) also deserves particular attention, as do the customer files in a [CRM]({{< relref "what-is-a-crm.md" >}}).

For each online tool, it is worth checking three points: is two-factor authentication available, who has access to what, and how can data be recovered if the vendor has a problem. ANSSI, the French national cybersecurity agency, also publishes best-practice guides for small organisations.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What are the cybersecurity best practices for a small business?</summary>

The basics are enabling two-factor authentication, using unique passwords managed by a password manager, applying updates, backing up data regularly with an offline copy and training the team to recognise phishing.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What should you do after a cyberattack?</summary>

Isolate the affected devices from the network, do not pay a ransom without seeking advice, keep the evidence and get support. In France, the public platform cybermalveillance.gouv.fr directs businesses to suitable providers and procedures.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why is two-factor authentication important?</summary>

It adds a second check on top of the password, for example a code in an app. A stolen password is then no longer enough to access the account.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the 3-2-1 backup rule?</summary>

It is a widely used method: keep three copies of your data, on two different types of media, with one copy offsite or offline. It allows data to be restored even if one copy is hit by a failure or ransomware.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Does a data breach have to be reported?</summary>

When a breach affects personal data and poses a risk to individuals, the GDPR requires notifying the supervisory authority, the CNIL in France, within 72 hours of becoming aware of it. The people concerned must also be informed if the risk is high.

</details>
