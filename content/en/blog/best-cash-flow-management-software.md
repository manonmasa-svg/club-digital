## Why use cash flow management software

**Cash flow management software** connects to the company's bank accounts and pulls in transactions automatically. It sorts them by category (sales, salaries, rent, taxes, suppliers) and shows the up-to-date cash position, with no export or re-keying.

Its second role is forecasting. Using history and upcoming invoices, the software projects inflows and outflows over the following weeks or months. Management can see a cash shortfall coming before it happens.

The third role is simulation. Scenarios make it possible to test a new hire, an investment or a late payment from a large customer, and measure the effect on the future balance.

This tracking relies on the invoices issued: a reliable tool, such as those in the comparison of the [best invoicing software in France]({{< relref "best-invoicing-software-france.md" >}}), feeds a more accurate forecast.

- **The option to try** with no commitment.

Okimia is cash flow software for small and medium-sized businesses. As of 28 November 2026, the fygr.io address redirects to okimia.com. Its pricing page shows three plans, two of them with a public price, detailed on [Okimia's pricing page](https://www.okimia.com/fr/tarifs).

| Okimia plan | Price (excl. VAT, billed annually) | Stated target | Content checked |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | €69 per month | Very small businesses up to €2M revenue | 1 entity, 2 banks, 3,000 transactions, 3 scenarios, 1 user, AI forecasting |
| Pro | €104 per month | Single-entity SMEs up to €5M | 4 banks, 10,000 transactions, multi-currency, ERP API, 5 scenarios, daily and weekly views, 3 users, chat support |
| Enterprise | On quotation | Multi-entity | Multi-entity, unlimited volumes |

The page highlights an annual saving with annual billing: €356 per year on Starter and €536 per year on Pro. Payment and card modules are available on quotation. A free 7-day trial with no commitment is offered.

Okimia's main strength is how readable its offer is for an SME. The limits (banks, transactions, scenarios, users) are spelled out, so a company knows in advance which plan fits. The daily and weekly views of the Pro plan suit companies with tight cash.

The drawback is the entry price, higher than Trezy's, and the 2-bank limit on the Starter plan. A company working with three banks has to move to Pro.

## 2. Agicap: cash management for multi-entity groups

Agicap does not publish prices. As of 28 November 2026, its pricing page offers a custom price, obtained through a form that asks for revenue and number of banks, among other things.

The features presented cover a wide scope: multi-bank and multi-entity cash management, forecasting at several levels of granularity, intragroup flows, accounting automation, tracking of financing and investments, customer and supplier modules, payments and bank connectivity.

Agicap is aimed mainly at structured companies, with several entities and a finance team. For a small single-entity business, the lack of a public price means going through a sales conversation before knowing the cost.

Trezy is the most accessible entry point. The free plan shows what automated tracking looks like before paying. The limit is the single bank account on the Free and Starter plans: with two banks or more, Premium is required.

## 4. Qonto: cash flow forecasting inside the business account

Qonto is an online business account, not standalone cash flow software. As of 28 November 2026, its pricing page states that cash flow tracking and forecasting are included from the Smart plan, at €19 excl. VAT per month billed annually (€228 per year). The Basic plan, at €9 excl. VAT per month, does not mention them.

The features described are historical cash analysis, tracking of upcoming transactions, several scenario-based forecasts, predictive analysis and an interactive cash flow table where each flow line can be adjusted. Qonto offers a free 1-month trial.

This option suits very small businesses whose main bank is already Qonto. It avoids adding another tool. To track several banks or entities, dedicated software remains a better fit.

## How to choose cash flow management software in 5 steps

1. **List the banks and entities** to connect. This figure rules out some plans straight away.
2. **Set the forecast horizon**: a few weeks for tight cash, 12 months or more for an annual budget.
3. **Check integrations** with the accounting and invoicing tools already in place.
4. **Compare the annual cost**, not just the monthly price shown, which often assumes a one-year commitment.
5. **Test with real data** during the free trial: 7 days at Okimia and Trezy, 1 month at Qonto.

These tools are online software billed by subscription. How they work and how they are priced is explained in the guide [What is SaaS software?]({{< relref "what-is-saas-software.md" >}}).

### Mistakes to avoid

- **Confusing bank balance with available cash**: upcoming social charges, VAT and loan repayments must be in the forecast.
- **Neglecting categorisation**: a forecast is only reliable if past transactions are correctly sorted. The first weeks need checking.
- **Choosing a plan that is too tight**: going over the bank or transaction limit forces a plan change mid-year.
- **Keeping a spreadsheet alongside**: two sources of figures end up diverging.

## Is a spreadsheet still an option?

A spreadsheet is enough for a very small business with one account and few flows. It is free and fully customisable.

Its limits show quickly: manual entry of transactions, risk of formula errors, no automatic update from the bank. As soon as the company has several accounts or cash becomes a management topic, dedicated software saves time and improves reliability.
