## Email marketing and automation: what a small business should expect from a tool

Choosing the **best email marketing tool for a small business** starts with one question: does the company only want to send a newsletter, or also trigger automatic messages? The two uses do not cost the same.

**Email marketing** means sending one-off campaigns to a contact list: a newsletter, a product announcement, an event invitation.

**Marketing automation** sends messages triggered by an event: a form sign-up, a first purchase, an abandoned cart, no opens for several months. Once the workflow is built, it runs on its own.

An email marketing tool does not replace a CRM: the difference is explained in the article [CRM: definition, features and business uses]({{< relref "what-is-a-crm.md" >}}). Many small businesses use both, connected to each other.

### The criteria used for the comparison

- **The free plan** and its real limits.
- **The entry price** and how it increases (contacts or sending volume).
- **The level of automation** available at each tier.
- **The number of users** included.
- **Transactional emails** (order confirmation, password reset), included or not.

## Brevo: the most complete email and automation tool to get started

Brevo describes itself on its pricing page as a 100% French marketing platform. The tool combines email marketing, SMS, WhatsApp, marketing automation and transactional emails. Plan details are on [Brevo's pricing page](https://www.brevo.com/pricing/).

Its pricing model stands out from the other three: Brevo charges by the volume of emails sent, not by the number of contacts. A small business with a large list that sends little therefore does not pay for its contact base.

According to the check made on 9 November 2026, the free plan allows up to 300 emails per day, without a credit card. The Starter plan costs €7 per month billed monthly, or €6.33 per month billed annually, from 5,000 emails per month. The Standard plan, shown as the most popular, costs €15 per month billed monthly, or €13.50 billed annually.

### Brevo's key features

- **Marketing automation from the free plan**: the detailed feature table shows workflows limited to 2,000 contacts on Free and Starter, then unlimited from Standard.
- **Transactional emails included** in every plan, with API and SMTP relay, according to the pricing page FAQ.
- **Standard** adds A/B testing, advanced reports, optimised send time, website behaviour tracking and one landing page.
- **No commitment**: the page states a choice between monthly and annual billing, and cancellation at any time from the account.
- **Prepaid credits** available for occasional needs, with no expiry date.
- **Limits**: a single user on Free and Starter, up to 3 on Standard through an add-on. The Pro plan, with 10 users, rises to €499 per month.

For a small business that wants to start without a budget and then automate its sending without switching tools, Brevo covers the longest path at the lowest price of the four.

## Comparative analysis: Mailchimp, ActiveCampaign and MailerLite against Brevo

### Mailchimp, the long-standing reference

Mailchimp, owned by Intuit, has four tiers. According to its pricing page in euros, checked on 9 November 2026 for 0 to 500 contacts: Essentials from €11.74 per month, Standard from €18.06 per month and Premium from €316.05 per month, with a promotional price of €268.64 per month for 12 months. The free plan is reserved for accounts with fewer than 250 contacts.

Automation depends heavily on the plan: journeys are limited to 4 steps on Essentials, compared with 200 steps on Standard. Essentials includes 5,000 emails per month and 3 users, Standard 6,000 emails and 5 users. A 14-day free trial is available on Essentials and Standard.

The page states that sending is paused if the contact or email limit is exceeded on the free plan, and that additional fees apply on paid plans.

### ActiveCampaign, the automation specialist

ActiveCampaign is built first and foremost for marketing automation. Its pricing page, checked in euros for 1,000 contacts billed annually, shows Starter at €15, Plus at €49, Pro at €79 and Enterprise at €145 per month.

Multi-step automation is included from Starter, with 10,000 emails per month and 1 user. Multichannel marketing and generative AI come with Plus. Predictive sending and conditional content are reserved for Pro and Enterprise.

ActiveCampaign has no permanent free plan: a 14-day trial is offered, without a credit card. It is the most advanced tool for building complex workflows, but also the most expensive as soon as several users are needed.

### MailerLite, simplicity at a low price

MailerLite shows prices in euros for 500 subscribers: Comfort at €11 per month billed monthly (€9.90 annually) and Power at €19 per month (€17.10 annually). The free plan covers 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails per month, with 2 users.

Comfort includes 5,000 emails per month, 50 active automations and 3 users. Power moves to unlimited emails, automations and users. On its pricing page, MailerLite states that its data storage centre is located in the European Union and is ISO 27001 certified.

The free plan is limited to 3 active automations of up to 5 steps. MailerLite suits a small list well, but the bill grows with the number of subscribers.

## GDPR: the email marketing rules a small business must follow in France

Whatever the tool, the sending rules stay the same. According to the [CNIL page on electronic marketing](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/la-prospection-commerciale-par-courrier-electronique-sms-mms-et-automate-dappel) (in French, updated on 10 June 2026, consulted on 9 November 2026):

| Recipient | Rule |
|---|---|
| Consumer (B2C) | Prior consent, through a box that is not pre-ticked |
| Consumer who is already a customer | Possible exception for similar products or services from the same company |
| Business (B2B) | Prior information and the possibility to object |
| Everyone | Each email identifies the sender and allows an easy unsubscribe |

The CNIL states that accepting terms of use is not enough to obtain consent. The sign-up forms offered by the four tools must therefore be set up with a dedicated checkbox.

Email marketing accounts give access to the entire contact base. [Small business cybersecurity best practices]({{< relref "small-business-cybersecurity-best-practices.md" >}}) apply to them, starting with two-factor authentication and unique passwords.

## Who is each tool for? Recommendations by profile

### Small business starting with email marketing

Brevo is the most solid starting point: the free plan allows up to 300 emails per day and lets you test automation. Moving to Standard, at €15 per month, unlocks workflows with no contact limit without changing tools.

### Small business with a large contact base and few sends

Brevo's model, based on email volume, avoids paying for contacts that are rarely emailed. With Mailchimp, ActiveCampaign and MailerLite, the price rises with the size of the list, even if sending stays infrequent.

### Marketing team building complex journeys

ActiveCampaign offers the most advanced workflows, with predictive sending and conditional content on the Pro plan. The budget is higher, and the Starter plan includes only one user.

### Creator or small organisation publishing a newsletter

MailerLite remains simple and affordable for a list of a few hundred subscribers, with 3 users from Comfort.

## How to choose an email marketing tool in 5 steps

1. **Count your contacts and estimate your monthly sending volume**: this sets the real price.
2. **List the workflows to automate**: welcome, follow-up, abandoned cart, birthday.
3. **Check the number of users included** in the target plan.
4. **Plan the connections** with the CRM, the website and the online store.
5. **Test during the free plan or the trial** with a real workflow and a real list.

On the customer relationship side, the comparison of the [best CRM for a small business]({{< relref "best-crm-small-business.md" >}}) covers CRMs that connect to these tools.

### Mistakes to avoid

1. Comparing prices without considering the pricing basis (contacts or volume).
2. Overlooking the automation limits of entry-level plans.
3. Importing a contact list without a legal basis to contact it.
4. Paying for inactive contacts instead of cleaning the list regularly.
5. Relying on a promotional price without checking the rate after the first year.
